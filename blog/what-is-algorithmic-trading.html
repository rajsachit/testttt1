What is algorithmic trading? A beginner’s guide

How rule-based trading strategies work, what you need to run one in India, and how to get started with small capital and clear risk limits.

Algorithmic trading, often called algo trading, means using a computer program to place buy and sell orders based on rules you define in advance. Instead of watching charts and clicking buttons, you decide the conditions once, and the system acts on them every time they are met.

For a long time this was limited to banks and large funds. Today retail traders in India can run rule-based strategies through their broker, often starting with a few thousand rupees in margin.

What algorithmic trading means

At its core, an algo is a set of instructions: when to enter, how much to buy or sell, when to exit, and what to do if something goes wrong. A simple example:

Example rule set Entry: Buy NIFTY futures when the 20-day moving average crosses above the 50-day moving average. Size: 1 lot per signal. Exit: Sell when the averages cross back, or if the loss reaches 1% of capital.

Because the rules are written down, they can be tested on historical data, checked for errors and run without emotion getting in the way.

How an algo works

Every automated strategy follows the same loop, whether it trades once a week or several times a minute.

  1. 1Read market data. Prices, volumes and indicators stream in from the exchange through your broker.
  2. 2Check the rules. The program compares live data with your entry and exit conditions.
  3. 3Place the order. When a condition is met, an order is sent to the broker with the size and price you set.
  4. 4Manage the position. Stop-losses, targets and daily loss limits are applied until the trade closes.

Common strategy types

Most retail algos fall into a handful of families. Each suits different market conditions.

Trend followingBuy strength, sell weaknessMarkets move steadily in one direction

Options sellingCollect premium as time decaysVolatility is stable or falling

What you need to start in India

  • A broker account with API access. Most large Indian brokers offer APIs that let approved platforms place orders on your behalf.
  • A platform to run the strategy. This can be your own code or a platform like ALGORICK that hosts tested algos and connects to your broker.
  • Trading capital and margin. Equity strategies can start small; futures and options need margin as set by the exchange.
  • A clear understanding of the rules. SEBI regulates algo trading for retail investors, and orders must go through registered brokers and approved platforms.
info

Check that any platform you use works with a SEBI-registered broker and that you understand who controls your orders and funds. Your money should always stay in your own broker account.

Risks to understand

Automation removes hesitation, but it does not remove risk. The most common problems beginners face are:

  • Overfitting. A strategy tuned too closely to past data can look excellent in a backtest and fail in live markets.
  • Changing conditions. A trend strategy can lose steadily in a sideways market.
  • Technical failures. Internet drops, API errors or broker outages can leave positions open.
  • Leverage. Futures and options magnify both gains and losses.
Judge a strategy by its worst drawdown, not its best month.

Getting started with small capital

A careful first month matters more than a fast one. A sensible path looks like this:

  • Pick one strategy and read how it enters, exits and limits losses.
  • Review its backtest, paying attention to maximum drawdown and losing streaks.
  • Paper trade or deploy with the smallest size for two to four weeks.
  • Set a daily loss limit you are comfortable with before going live.
  • Scale up only after live results match what you expected.
